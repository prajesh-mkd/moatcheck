# Y Combinator Insights — Patterns from the World's Top Accelerator

## YC by the Numbers
- **Founded:** 2005 by Paul Graham
- **Total companies funded:** 4,000+ (as of 2025)
- **Combined valuation of YC companies:** $600B+
- **Notable alumni:** Airbnb ($75B), Stripe ($95B), DoorDash ($60B), Coinbase ($50B), Reddit ($15B), Instacart ($10B), Dropbox ($8B), Twitch (acquired $970M)

## Paul Graham's Key Startup Advice

### "Do things that don't scale"
- In the early days, manually recruit users, hand-hold customers, personally onboard everyone
- Airbnb went door-to-door photographing listings
- Stripe's founders physically installed their software in users' offices ("the Collison Installation")
- This unsexy work builds relationships and understanding that competitors can't replicate

### "Make something people want"
- The simplest and most ignored advice. 42% of startups fail because they build something nobody wants.
- Talk to users BEFORE building. If you can't find 10 people who desperately want what you're building, stop.

### "It's better to have 100 users who love you than 1,000 who kind of like you"
- Depth of engagement > breadth of users
- "Love" users become evangelists. "Like" users churn.

## What YC Looks For in Founders

1. **Determination** — #1 quality. "We'd rather fund a team with no idea but great determination than brilliant founders who give up easily."
2. **Domain expertise** — Do you know this problem space better than anyone? Have you lived it?
3. **Technical ability** — At least one founder should be able to build the product. No "idea people." 
4. **Speed of execution** — YC companies ship FAST. Most ship v1 in <30 days during the batch.
5. **Honesty** — "Founders who are honest about what's not working fix things faster."

## Common Traits of YC Success Stories

1. **Founder-market fit** — Founders had deep personal experience with the problem
2. **Started narrow** — Dominated a tiny niche before expanding
3. **Built for themselves** — Solved their own problem first
4. **Iterated fast** — Shipped weekly, talked to users daily
5. **Had a technical co-founder** — 90%+ of successful YC companies have a technical co-founder

## YC Advice on Moats

- "The best moat is one that gets stronger as you grow" — Sam Altman
- "Network effects are the strongest moat. If you can build them, do it." — Paul Graham
- "Most startups don't need a moat on day one. They need users who love them. The moat comes from doing things others won't copy because they seem too small." — Michael Seibel
- "The company that talks to users the most wins. Not the smartest team, not the most funded team — the team that listens hardest." — Dalton Caldwell

## Typical YC Company Journey

1. **Pre-YC:** Working prototype, maybe a few users
2. **During batch (3 months):** Ship aggressively, talk to users, iterate weekly, prepare for Demo Day
3. **Demo Day:** Pitch to 1,000+ investors. Typical raise: $1.5-3M
4. **Post-YC Year 1:** Find product-market fit, reach $500K-1M ARR
5. **Series A (18-24 months post):** $1-3M ARR, raise $10-20M

## YC's Most Surprising Successes

- **Airbnb:** Rejected by most investors. "Who would stay in a stranger's home?"
- **Dropbox:** "It's just file storage." Investors didn't see the need.
- **Stripe:** "Payments are solved." Both founders were teenagers when they started.
- **Reddit:** Launched as a single page of links. No profiles, no subreddits.

These all succeeded because founders persisted through universal skepticism and found passionate early users.
