# Startup Failure Case Studies

## Consumer & Marketplace Failures

### Quibi (2020) — Lost $1.75B
- **What:** Short-form premium video streaming for mobile
- **Raised:** $1.75 billion from major Hollywood studios
- **Why it failed:** Launched during COVID (people watched TV at home, not mobile). No social sharing features. Content wasn't differentiated enough from YouTube/TikTok. Priced at $5-8/month competing with free content. Shut down after 6 months.
- **Lesson:** Timing matters enormously. Also, you can't outspend your way into a market that doesn't want your product.

### Juicero (2017) — Lost $120M
- **What:** $400 WiFi-connected juicer that squeezed proprietary juice packs
- **Raised:** $120M from Google Ventures, Kleiner Perkins
- **Why it failed:** Bloomberg revealed you could squeeze the packs by hand — the machine was unnecessary. Over-engineered solution to a non-problem. DRM on juice is absurd.
- **Lesson:** If your product can be replaced by bare hands, you have no moat.

### Pets.com (2000) — Lost $300M
- **What:** Online pet supply retailer
- **Raised:** $300M, went public
- **Why it failed:** Shipping heavy, low-margin pet food was economically impossible. Spent $179 to acquire each customer, average order was $35. Spent lavishly on Super Bowl ads. Shut down 9 months after IPO.
- **Lesson:** Unit economics must work. If you lose money on every order, volume makes it worse.

### MoviePass (2019) — Lost $600M+
- **What:** $10/month unlimited movie ticket subscription
- **Raised:** Parent company Helios invested $600M+
- **Why it failed:** Paid full price for tickets ($10-15 each) while charging $10/month. Users saw 3+ movies/month. Burned through cash instantly. Changed terms repeatedly, losing trust.
- **Lesson:** A business model where your costs scale faster than revenue is a death sentence.

### Vine (2017) — Acquired then shut down
- **What:** 6-second looping video platform (owned by Twitter)
- **Why it failed:** Twitter neglected it. Didn't monetize for creators. Instagram and Snapchat copied the format with better tools. Top creators left for YouTube. Had 200M users but couldn't retain them.
- **Lesson:** Without creator monetization, your platform is just a launching pad to somewhere else.

## Tech & SaaS Failures

### Theranos (2018) — Lost $700M+, criminal fraud
- **What:** Blood testing with just a finger prick
- **Raised:** $700M+, valued at $9B
- **Why it failed:** The technology didn't work. Results were inaccurate and dangerous. Founder Elizabeth Holmes committed fraud. Classic case of faking it without making it.
- **Lesson:** You can't fake product-market fit. Deep tech requires actual breakthroughs.

### Fast (2022) — Lost $120M
- **What:** One-click checkout for e-commerce
- **Raised:** $120M from Stripe
- **Why it failed:** Spent $10M+/month with only ~$600K in annual revenue. Hired 400+ employees before product-market fit. Stripe, Shopify, and Bolt already dominated checkout.
- **Lesson:** Hiring ahead of revenue is the fastest way to die. No moat against well-funded incumbents.

### Jawbone (2017) — Lost $930M
- **What:** Fitness trackers and wearable technology
- **Raised:** $930M total
- **Why it failed:** Quality issues plagued products. Fitbit and Apple Watch outpaced them. Tried to pivot to health-focused B2B but ran out of cash. Couldn't compete on manufacturing scale.
- **Lesson:** Hardware is brutal. You need cost advantages and manufacturing moats that software companies don't need.

### WeWork IPO Collapse (2019) — Lost $40B in value
- **What:** Flexible coworking/office space
- **Raised:** $12B+ from SoftBank
- **Why it failed (initially):** Filed for IPO at $47B valuation, withdrawn after scrutiny of massive losses ($1.6B/year), corporate governance issues, and founder's erratic behavior. Eventually went public at $9B, then bankrupt.
- **Lesson:** Revenue without profit is just expensive popularity.

### Munchery (2019) — Lost $125M
- **What:** Chef-prepared meal delivery
- **Raised:** $125M
- **Why it failed:** Food delivery has razor-thin margins. Customer acquisition costs were unsustainable. Competed with DoorDash, UberEats, and home cooking. Food spoilage added waste costs.
- **Lesson:** Food delivery is a graveyard. You need incredible logistics moats to survive.

## Fintech & Crypto Failures

### FTX (2022) — Lost $32B, criminal fraud
- **What:** Cryptocurrency exchange
- **Valued at:** $32B
- **Why it failed:** Misused customer funds, fraudulent accounting, no internal controls. Sam Bankman-Fried convicted of fraud.
- **Lesson:** Lack of regulatory moat and governance is a catastrophic risk in fintech.

### Wealthsimple Cash / Numerous small fintech failures
- **Pattern:** Dozens of challenger banks and fintech startups failed because they couldn't compete with incumbent banks' regulatory moats, trust, and deposit insurance.

## Delivery & Logistics Failures

### Webvan (2001) — Lost $830M
- **What:** Online grocery delivery (same-day)
- **Raised:** $830M, went public
- **Why it failed:** Built massive automated warehouses before proving demand. Expansion to 8 cities simultaneously. Spent $1B building infrastructure. Only had 47,000 orders/day across all markets.
- **Lesson:** Capital-intensive businesses need to prove unit economics in one market before expanding.

### Kozmo.com (2001) — Lost $280M
- **What:** Free 1-hour delivery of convenience items
- **Raised:** $280M
- **Why it failed:** Free delivery on $5 items is economically insane. No delivery fee, no minimum order. Gross margins were deeply negative. Tried to copy Amazon's "grow first, profit later" but without Amazon's scale advantages.
- **Lesson:** Free + fast + cheap = bankrupt. Pick two at most.
